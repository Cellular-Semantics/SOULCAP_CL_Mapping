# Gamma-Delta T Cell and MAIT Cell Marker Literature Support

Evidence for SOULCAP γδ T cell and MAIT cell marker definitions, extracted verbatim from open-access papers. All blockquotes are copied character-for-character from the source. Paywalled papers (OMIP-084, OMIP-020, Nature Immunology 2023) are noted in the Sources table but no quotes are drawn from them.

---

## Part I: Gamma-Delta T Cell Markers

### CD3 and TCRγδ (required — pan-γδ T cell markers)

Context: γδ T cells express CD3 and the γδ form of the TCR. The two T cell lineages — αβ and γδ — are mutually exclusive at the TCR level, so TCRγδ staining with CD3 uniquely marks this lineage.

> "Cytobank 5.0 was used (following quality control and data scaling) to gate live/CD3+/γδ TCR+ cells."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "As γδ T cells are less likely to cause graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) than their αβ counterparts"
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "γδ T cells are a highly versatile immune lineage involved in host defense and homeostasis, but questions remain around their heterogeneity, precise function and role during health and disease."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

---

### γδ T cell subsets — overview (three Vδ populations)

Context: SOULCAP defines Vδ1, Vδ2, Vδ3, Vγ9, Vγ9Vδ1, Vγ9Vδ2, and pan-γδ populations. The three-subset Vδ-based classification is standard in the literature.

> "The γδ T cells are typically classified into three primary subsets based on their δ chain usage: Vδ1+, Vδ2+, and Vδ3+"
— Laub et al. 2025 (PMID:40746558; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2025.1618393)

> "Traditionally, γδ T cells existed as three populations based on δ chain expression. Vδ1 cells predominantly reside in mucosal sites and epithelia, while Vδ2 cells comprise 50–90% of γδ T cells in the circulation."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

---

### TCR Vδ2 / TRDV2 (major peripheral blood γδ subset marker)

Context: Vδ2-expressing cells — usually paired with Vγ9 — are the dominant γδ T cell subset in peripheral blood of healthy adults. Identification of the SOULCAP "Vd2" cell type (non-Vγ9 Vδ2) requires TCR Vδ2+ combined with Vγ9- and Vδ1-.

> "Vδ2 cells comprise 50–90% of γδ T cells in the circulation and are both effectors and antigen-presenting cells (APCs)."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "The most abundant T cell subtype in human peripheral blood is the Vδ2+ T cell."
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "It accounts for approximately 50% to 95% of total γδ T cells"
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "Vδ2+ T cells, represented by Vγ9Vδ2 T cells, the dominant circulating subset in humans"
— Laub et al. 2025 (PMID:40746558; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2025.1618393)

> "Vδ1− cells are likely to be the predominant Vδ2+ in circulation"
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

---

### TCR Vγ9 / TRGV9 (paired with Vδ2 in the dominant peripheral blood subset)

Context: The Vγ9Vδ2 heterodimer is the defining TCR of the dominant blood γδ T cell subset. SOULCAP uses TCR Vγ9 positivity as a required marker for the "Vg9 Vd2" and "Vg9" cell types, and optionally ("+/-") for "Vd1".

> "Most predominantly, it pairs with the Vγ9 chain forming a functional heterodimer, Therefore, this cell is also known as Vγ9Vδ2 T cell"
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "Vδ2+ γδ T cells, the dominant circulating subset in humans, are activated by non-peptidic phosphorylated intermediates of the mevalonate pathway."
— Laub et al. 2025 (PMID:40746558; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2025.1618393)

---

### TCR Vδ1 / TRDV1 (tissue-resident subset; paired with various Vγ chains)

Context: Vδ1+ cells predominate in mucosal and epithelial tissues and are the minor γδ T cell subset in blood. SOULCAP defines "Vd1" (TCRgd+ TCR V delta 1+, V delta 2-, V gamma 9+/-) and "Vg9 Vd1" (both V delta 1+ and V gamma 9+).

> "Vδ1+ T cells are mainly found in the mucosal tissues (intestinal tissues) and epithelial tissues (such as skin)"
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "Vδ1+ T cells are the primary tissue-resident γδ T cell subset"
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "The Vδ1 chain can pair with multiple γ chains (e.g., Vγ2, Vγ3, Vγ4)"
— Wang et al. 2026 (PMID:42344895; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1827307)

> "Vδ1+ cells maintain a predominant population of TEM/TEMRA γδ T cells"
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "Vδ1+ and Vδ1− (as a percentage of γδ T cells) of 30.6% (± SD 27%) and 69.2% (± SD 27%) respectively."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

---

### CD161 (NKR-P1 / KLRB1 — used to define CD161+ γδ T cell subsets)

Context: CD161 (also called NKR-P1 or KLRB1) is differentially expressed across γδ T cell subsets and serves as a key marker distinguishing subpopulations, particularly Vδ1- (Vδ2-enriched) from Vδ1+ cells.

> "γδ T cells were divided into two populations based on Vδ1 expression. Each population was subgated based on CD45RA and CD27 expression to differentiate naïve and memory cells and then gated based on CD161, CD4 and CD8 expression."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "Comparison of CD161 expression between naïve and memory Vδ1+ and Vδ1− populations revealed a significant difference in both TEM cells and TCM cells (p < 0.0001), with Vδ1− cells having the highest surface expression of CD161."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "Both methods show CD161 as a primary differentiating phenotypic marker for Vδ cell subsets."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "Overall, the pattern of marker expression on the eight clusters show that CD161 plays a central role in the differentiation of γδ T cell subtypes."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "CD161 expression on γδ T cells is associated with enhanced IFN-γ and IL-17 production and enhanced endothelial transmigration."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

> "CD161 is a C-type lectin-like membrane receptor expressed at high levels on NK, NKT, Th17, Mucosa Associated Invariant T cells (MAITs), non-MAIT CD8+, tissue-resident memory cells (TRMs)."
— Karunathilaka et al. 2022 (PMID:35193613; DOI:10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w)

## Part II: MAIT Cell Markers

### CD3 and TCRαβ (required positive — MAIT cells are αβ T cells)

Context: MAIT cells are a subset of αβ T cells. They express CD3 as part of the TCR complex, and their TCR comprises αβ chains (TRAV1-2/TRAJ33 α-chain paired with restricted TRBV β-chains), not γδ chains.

> "Human MAIT cells express an invariant TCRα chain comprising TRAV1-2 joined to TRAJ33 with a restricted TCRβ chain TRBV6 and TRBV20."
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

> "demonstrated the flow cytometry using CD3, CD4, CD8, TCRβ, MR1 tetramer"
— Cai et al. 2026 (PMID:41804231; DOI:10.1111/cpr.70194)

> "MAIT cells belong to a population of 'unconventional' T cells which also include gamma delta (gd) T cells and invariant natural killer (iNKT) cells."
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

---

### TCR Vα7.2 / TRAV1-2 (required — invariant α-chain of MAIT TCR)

Context: The invariant TRAV1-2 (Vα7.2) gene segment defines the MAIT cell α-chain in humans. Vα7.2 staining is the primary surrogate marker used alongside CD161 in flow cytometry panels. The alias "Vα7.2" refers to the protein product of the TRAV1-2 gene.

> "the semi-invariant conserved MAIT TCR predominantly utilizes TRAV1-2 (Vα7.2 in humans)"
— Laub et al. 2025 (PMID:40746558; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2025.1618393)

> "their semi-invariant TCR typically comprises a concrete TCRα chain (encoded by *TRAV1-2/TRAJ33* genes)"
— García-Escribano et al. 2026 (PMID:42433370; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1852383)

> "In humans, the majority of MAIT cells are defined as CD8+CD161+Vα7.2+ T cells"
— García-Escribano et al. 2026 (PMID:42433370; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1852383)

> "Circulating MAIT cells from healthy donors can be identified using surrogate set of markers TCRVα7.2+ CD161+ (and/or CD26+)."
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

> "Using TCR Vα7.2 and CD161, we identified MAIT cells in human liver tissues"
— Cai et al. 2026 (PMID:41804231; DOI:10.1111/cpr.70194)

---

### CD161 (required positive — pan-MAIT marker; co-expressed with Vα7.2)

Context: CD161 (encoded by KLRB1) is expressed on essentially all MAIT cells and is used alongside Vα7.2 to identify bona fide MAIT cells. A caveat is that CD161 can also mark other innate-like T cell populations (NKT, Th17, some CD8+ T cells) and can be downregulated upon activation, so it must be used in combination with Vα7.2 or MR1 tetramer.

> "Human MAIT cells were gated as Zombie−CD14−CD19−CD8α+CD161+Vα7.2+ cells"
— García-Escribano et al. 2026 (PMID:42433370; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1852383)

> "Gene transcriptomic analysis has identified MAIT cell-specific markers, including TRAV1-2+ TCR, CD161, PLZF, and IL-18Rα"
— Laub et al. 2025 (PMID:40746558; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2025.1618393)

> "Surrogate markers, especially CD161 may lead to the inconsistent identification of MAIT cells, as CD161 can be downregulated in activation."
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

> "annotated cluster 2 into MAIT according to the high expression of KLRB1, SLC4A10, ZBTB16, RORA, and RORC"
— Cai et al. 2026 (PMID:41804231; DOI:10.1111/cpr.70194)

---

### MR1 Tetramer (alternative MAIT identification; more specific than Vα7.2 alone)

Context: Tetramers of MR1 loaded with 5-OP-RU (a riboflavin metabolite) bind directly to the MAIT TCR and are more specific than Vα7.2 staining alone. MR1 tetramers identify MAIT cells regardless of CD161 expression level. The ligands for MR1 include microbial riboflavin metabolites, folate derivatives, and other small molecules.

> "The most specific method of identifying MAIT cells is by using MR1-5-OP-RU tetramers in flow cytometry."
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

> "MAIT cells were isolated from PBMCs using a 5-OP-RU-loaded PE-conjugated MR1 tetramer"
— García-Escribano et al. 2026 (PMID:42433370; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2026.1852383)

> "performed longitudinal monitoring of circulating MAIT cells using MR1‐tetramer staining"
— Cai et al. 2026 (PMID:41804231; DOI:10.1111/cpr.70194)

> "The MAIT cell TCR recognizes a series of MR1-binding ligands which includes endogenous bile acids, drug molecules, folate, and riboflavin derivatives"
— Azad et al. 2025 (PMID:41511352; DOI:10.3390/cells15010069)

## Markers with no direct evidence found

- **TCR V delta 3 (TRDV3) — Vδ3 T cells**: Vδ3 is enumerated as a subset category (e.g., "Vδ1+, Vδ2+, and Vδ3+" in Laub et al. 2025) but no open-access paper was found that provides verbatim gating or phenotyping criteria for the Vδ3 population specifically. SOULCAP defines Vδ3 by exclusion: TCRgd+ Vδ1- Vδ2- Vγ9-.
- **TCR V gamma 9 in Vδ1 context (Vγ9Vδ1 pairing)**: The literature confirms Vδ1 pairs with multiple Vγ chains (Vγ2, Vγ3, Vγ4; Wang et al. 2026) and that Vδ2 pairs predominantly with Vγ9 (Wang et al. 2026). No open-access quote was found explicitly characterising the Vγ9Vδ1 minor subset, which SOULCAP lists as a separate cell type.
- **TCRαβ exclusion on γδ T cells (explicit)**: The literature confirms γδ T cells are the αβ counterpart's alternative lineage (Karunathilaka et al. 2022: "less likely to cause GvHD than their αβ counterparts"), and SOULCAP's TCRgd panel gates on TCRγδ+ or TCRαβ- to exclude αβ T cells, but no open-access paper was found with a verbatim gating statement using "TCRαβ-" to define γδ T cells.
- **CD26 as supplementary MAIT marker**: Mentioned in one paper as an optional co-marker ("TCRVα7.2+ CD161+ (and/or CD26+)"; Azad et al. 2025) but not included in SOULCAP's current MAIT definition.

# NK Cell Marker Literature Support

Evidence for SOULCAP NK cell marker definitions, extracted verbatim from three open-access papers.
Two papers (Vivier 2024 Nature PMID:38383621; Aranami 2006 J Immunol PMID:17015755) are paywalled and could not be accessed.

---

## Markers with evidence

### CD56 (positive marker)

NK cells are canonically defined by surface CD56 expression. CD56 is the 140-kDa isoform of the neural cell adhesion molecule (NCAM).

> "Natural killer cells are prototypic members of the innate lymphoid cell (ILC) family and characterized in humans by expression of the phenotypic marker CD56 in the absence of CD3."
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

> "human NK cells are typically divided into two main categories on the basis of the density of CD56, the 140-kDa isoform of the neural cell adhesion molecule (NCAM)2, on the cell surface: CD56bright and CD56dim NK cells."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

---

### CD3 (exclusion marker)

NK cells lack CD3 surface expression; this distinguishes them from T cells and is part of the standard lineage-exclusion gate used to identify ILCs including NK cells.

> "Natural killer cells are prototypic members of the innate lymphoid cell (ILC) family and characterized in humans by expression of the phenotypic marker CD56 in the absence of CD3."
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

> "ILCs are identified as “lineage” (Lin) negative lymphocytes, lacking expression of surface markers more specifically expressed on T cells (CD3, CD5, TCR), B cells (CD19, CD20, BCR), myelomonocytic cells (CD14, CD15, CD36), and dendritic cells (DCs) (CD116, CD123, CD303)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

The CD56-negative NK cell subpopulation is explicitly defined by CD3 absence:

> "there also exists a CD56− NK cell subpopulation, namely CD3−CD4−CD14−CD19−CD16+NKp46+ lymphocytes"
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

---

### CD19 (exclusion marker)

CD19 marks B cells and is excluded from the NK cell lineage gate.

> "ILCs lack expression of markers specific for other leukocytes, including antigen-specific T and B cell receptors (i.e., CD3/TCR and CD19/BCR)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

> "ILCs are identified as “lineage” (Lin) negative lymphocytes, lacking expression of surface markers more specifically expressed on T cells (CD3, CD5, TCR), B cells (CD19, CD20, BCR), myelomonocytic cells (CD14, CD15, CD36), and dendritic cells (DCs) (CD116, CD123, CD303)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

The CD56-negative NK cell subpopulation definition also uses CD19 exclusion:

> "there also exists a CD56− NK cell subpopulation, namely CD3−CD4−CD14−CD19−CD16+NKp46+ lymphocytes"
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

### CD14 (exclusion marker)

CD14 marks monocytes and is excluded from the NK cell lineage gate.

> "ILCs are identified as “lineage” (Lin) negative lymphocytes, lacking expression of surface markers more specifically expressed on T cells (CD3, CD5, TCR), B cells (CD19, CD20, BCR), myelomonocytic cells (CD14, CD15, CD36), and dendritic cells (DCs) (CD116, CD123, CD303)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

> "there also exists a CD56− NK cell subpopulation, namely CD3−CD4−CD14−CD19−CD16+NKp46+ lymphocytes"
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

### CD127 / IL-7Rα (negative or low on mature NK cells)

CD127 (IL-7Rα) is expressed on all non-NK innate lymphoid cells (ILC1, ILC2, ILC3) and is used to distinguish mature NK cells from other ILCs. NK cells can express low levels of CD127, but it is a characteristic marker of non-NK ILCs rather than NK cells.

> "In humans, all non-NK ILCs express CD127 (IL-7Rα) and CD161 (NKRP1A) (13–16), and they may be further distinguished according to the expression of other subset-associated surface antigens including CXCR3, CD294 (CRTH2), and CD117 (c-Kit) for ILC1s, ILC2s, and ILC3s, respectively (10, 17). Human NK cells can also express the pan-ILC markers CD127 and CD161 (18–20), but NK cells are typically distinguished by their surface expression of CD16 (FcγRIIIA), CD94/NKG2 heterodimers, killer immunoglobulin-like receptors (KIRs), NKG2D, and NKp80 (21)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

CD127 expression also distinguishes NK cell progenitors from ILC3 precursors in secondary lymphoid tissues:

> "They demonstrated that a minute CD127− fraction preferentially gave rise to NK cells whereas the predominant CD127+ fraction mostly retained ILC3 features. Thus CD127 has since been touted as a critical marker distinguishing human stage 3 NK cell precursors from ILC3s"
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

*Note:* These papers confirm that non-NK ILCs reliably express CD127 while mature NK cells typically do not. The CD127-low/negative phenotype of mature NK cells supports its use as a lineage-exclusion marker for ILC contamination, though the cited passages describe NK cells as variably expressing CD127 rather than being uniformly CD127-negative. Use of CD127- as a required exclusion warrants caution.

### CD56bright vs CD56dim subsets

The existence and phenotypic properties of two major NK cell subsets defined by CD56 density are firmly established.

> "They are usually further divided into two subsets based on their surface level expression of CD56 (3). Whereas most NK cells in peripheral blood are CD56dim, CD56bright NK cells are more abundant in tissues."
— Van Acker HH et al. 2017 (PMID:28791027; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00892)

> "Two subsets of human peripheral blood (PB) NK cells can be distinguished according to their relative expression of the pan-NK cell surface marker, CD56 (NCAM-1): “CD56bright” and “CD56dim” (22, 23). CD56bright human NK cells express high levels of CD62L (l-selectin) and CD94 but absent or low levels of CD16 and KIRs; they predominate in secondary lymphoid tissues (SLTs) such as lymph nodes (LNs) and tonsils; they show low baseline perforin expression and cytotoxic activity ex vivo; and they rapidly produce cytokines, including IFN-γ, following stimulation by monocyte-derived cytokines (“monokines”), such as IL-12, IL-15, and IL-18 (22). In contrast, CD56dim NK cells are CD16hi and express more KIRs but less CD94 and CD62L; they predominate in PB; they show high baseline perforin expression and cytotoxicity against MHC class I negative target cells; and they preferentially produce cytokines in response to direct target cell interactions rather than via monokine stimulation (3)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

High-dimensional CITE-seq and scRNA-seq analysis confirms the CD56bright/dim dichotomy corresponds to the NK2 and NK1 major clusters:

> "The NK2 cluster was defined by high expression of CD56, CD27, CD44, CD54, CD45RB, CD314 (NKG2D) and CD335 (NKp46) and little or no expression of CD16 and CD57 at the protein level (Fig. 1c,d)."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

---

### CD16 (expressed on CD56dim NK cells; absent/low on CD56bright)

CD16 (FcγRIIIA) is a key NK cell marker expressed at high levels on the dominant peripheral blood CD56dim subset.

> "NK cells are typically distinguished by their surface expression of CD16 (FcγRIIIA), CD94/NKG2 heterodimers, killer immunoglobulin-like receptors (KIRs), NKG2D, and NKp80 (21)."
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

> "The NK1 cluster was marked by high protein expression of CD16, CX3CR1, CD161, β7-integrin and CD38 (Fig. 1c,d)."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

> "Expression of the classic NK cell markers CD57 and CD16 was reduced or absent on NK2 cells compared with NK1 and NK3 cells, indicating that the NK2 population comprised CD56bright and early-stage CD56dim NK cells."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

---

### CD57 (positive marker for mature NK subsets)

CD57 marks terminally differentiated, mature NK cells within the CD56dim compartment.

> "Further distinctions in the CD56dim population are made on the basis of expression of the CD57 carbohydrate moiety3 on the cell surface and the absence of CD94–NKG2A and CD62L; cells with these features comprise a more mature subset4–7."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

> "this cluster's preferential expression of CD57 and PRDM1 suggests that it also includes mature CD57+CD56dim NK cells that are not produced in response to HCMV."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

> "some that were NKp80+CD16+ and coexpressed KIRs and CD57, the latter of which has been associated with terminal maturation and may represent a putative “stage 6” of human NK cell development"
— Scoville SD et al. 2017 (PMID:28396671; DOI:10.3389/fimmu.2017.00360)

### HLA-DR (ideal exclusion; expressed on a subset of NK cells)

HLA-DR expression is not a universal feature of NK cells, but a subset of adaptive/HCMV-associated NK cells does express HLA-D-family genes, motivating its use as an ideal (non-required) exclusion marker in gating.

> "Notably, NK3B (cluster 1) was distinguished by expression of members of the HLA-D gene family, CD74, CCL5, CD7 and KLRC1, and NK3A exhibited enhanced cytotoxic capabilities (through expression of GZMA, GZMB and PRF1)"
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

*Note:* No passage in any of the three papers explicitly states that canonical NK cells are HLA-DR-negative. Van Acker et al. mention HLA-DR only as a generic activation marker comparable to CD69. The Rebuffet finding that a subset of adaptive NK cells (NK3B) expresses HLA-D family genes is the strongest available evidence from these papers that HLA-DR can be found on NK cells, which is why it is used as an ideal (not required) exclusion.

### CD11c (expressed on NK2 / CD56bright subset)

CD11c (ITGAX, integrin alpha X) shows differential expression across NK cell subsets, being more pronounced on NK2 (CD56bright-enriched) cells.

> "CD11C (ITGAX) expression was more pronounced in NK2."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

Full context:

> "Activation markers also served as differential markers, with CD69 being most prominent in NK1B and NKint, whereas TNFRSF18 (encoding GITR) was more pronounced in NK2. Moreover, classic markers of NK maturation aligned with earlier descriptions: CD56 (NCAM1) was more prevalent in NK2, and CD16 (FCGR3A) expression increased progressively from NK2 to NK1C. In addition, CD11B (ITGAM) levels were higher in NK1B, whereas CD11C (ITGAX) expression was more pronounced in NK2."
— Rebuffet L et al. 2024 (PMID:38956378; DOI:10.1038/s41590-024-01883-0)

*Note:* CD11c expression in NK cells is subset-specific. CD56bright NK cells (NK2) preferentially express CD11c; NK cells are not uniformly CD11c-negative. This supports using CD11c as an ideal exclusion rather than a required one — strict application as CD11c- would exclude CD56bright NK cells.

## Markers with no direct evidence found in these three papers

None of the ten SOULCAP markers listed above were completely absent from the three papers; all had at least indirect supporting evidence. However, the following caveats apply:

- **CD127 (required negative):** These papers show mature NK cells can variably express CD127; the "non-NK ILCs reliably express CD127" framing is clearer than "NK cells are CD127-negative." Direct evidence for mature peripheral blood NK cells being CD127-negative is not provided verbatim in any of these three papers.
- **HLA-DR (ideal exclusion):** No passage explicitly states that canonical NK cells are HLA-DR-negative. The Rebuffet NK3B finding shows a subset expresses HLA-D family genes, but this is transcriptomic data for an adaptive NK subset.

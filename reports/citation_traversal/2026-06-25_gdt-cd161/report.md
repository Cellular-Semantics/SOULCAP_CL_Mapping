# Surface markers defining human γδ T-cell subsets: roles of CD161 and HLA-DR

## Synthesis

Beyond the Vδ chain (Vδ1 vs Vδ1−, the latter likely Vδ2+/Vδ3+), the seed paper by Karunathilaka et al. (2022) used a focused flow-cytometry panel to resolve human peripheral-blood γδ T-cell subsets, with CD161 emerging as the principal differentiating marker and HLA-DR marking a distinct, APC-like fraction. A round-2 paper (Fonseca et al. 2020) corroborates that the immune-checkpoint molecule CD5 and the activation marker CD28 differ between Vδ1 and Vδ2 subsets.

### (a) The marker panel used to define subsets

Subsetting was anchored on Vδ1, then refined with CD45RA and CD27 (naïve/memory staging) and CD161, CD4, and CD8.

> "γδ T cells were divided into two populations based on Vδ1 expression. Each population was subgated based on CD45RA and CD27 expression to differentiate naïve and memory cells and then gated based on CD161, CD4 and CD8expression."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

The same channels, plus HLA-DR, were used for unsupervised clustering.

> "Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) analysis was performed using Vδ1, CD45RA, CD27, HLA-DR, CD161, CD4, and CD8 as clustering channels [21]."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

CD45RA and CD27 specifically defined the naïve/memory differentiation states within each Vδ population.

> "CD45RA and CD27 were used to gate CD45RA + CD27 + naïve (T N ), CD45RA − CD27 + central memory (T CM ), CD45RA − CD27 − effector memory (T EM ) and CD45RA + CD27 − terminally differentiated effector memory (T EMRA ) in the Vδ1 + and Vδ1 − populations (Fig. 1C&D)."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

### (b) The role of CD161

CD161 is the chief marker defining the novel naïve/memory subsets.

> "We used multi−parametric flow cytometry, dimensionality reduction, unsupervised clustering, and self-organizing maps (SOM) to identify novel γδ T cell naïve/memory subsets chiefly defined by CD161 expression levels, a surface membrane receptor that can be activating or suppressive."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

Gating on Vδ1 together with CD161 yielded four subsets.

> "Four distinct γδ T cell subsets were identified when gating on Vδ1 and CD161 (Fig. 1A), with enumerative means and ± SDs in Vδ1 − CD161 + cells (53% ± 30%), Vδ1 − CD161 − cells (12% ± 9%), Vδ1 + CD161 − (18% ± 23%), andVδ1 + CD161 + cells (16% ± 20%) determined."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

Both manual and unsupervised analyses confirm CD161 as a primary differentiating marker.

> "Both methods show CD161 as a primary differentiating phenotypic marker for Vδ cell subsets."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

Vδ1− cells carry the highest CD161 surface expression, distinguishing them functionally from Vδ1+ cells.

> "Comparison of CD161 expression between naïve and memory Vδ1 + and Vδ1 − populations revealed a significant difference in both T EM cells and T CM cells (p < 0.0001), with Vδ1 − cells having the highest surface expression of CD161 (Fig. 1E&F)."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

### (c) The role of HLA-DR

Combining Vδ1 with HLA-DR defined a second four-subset structure, identifying an HLA-DR+ fraction.

> "Four subsets were observed when combining Vδ1 and HLA-DR expression (Fig. 1B), with enumerative means and SDs in Vδ1 − HLA-DR − cells (54% ± 28%), Vδ1 − HLA-DR + cells (11% ± 13%), Vδ1 + HLA-DR − cells (27% ± 26%), and Vδ1 + HLA-DR + cells (8% ± 8) determined."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

The high HLA-DR expression implies an antigen-presenting-cell-like role for these subsets.

> "Such high HLA-DR expression on γδ T subsets implies the operation of complex APC machinery for adaptive system function."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

In clustering, HLA-DR distinguished separate islands/meta-clusters rather than serving as a primary axis.

> "Three smaller islands were also seen; a CD4 + island containing both Vδ1 + and Vδ1 − cells, and two very small HLA-DR + islands."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

### (d) Round-2 corroboration: CD5 and CD28 differ between Vδ1 and Vδ2 subsets

The seed paper points to differential CD5 and CD28 as support for divergent functional roles.

> "Recent data showing differential expression of immune checkpoint CD5 and activation marker CD28 on γδ T cell Vδ subsets supports this hypothesis [25]."
> — Karunathilaka et al. 2022, DOI: 10.1186/s12979-022-00269-w

Fonseca et al. (2020) directly measured this difference, showing lower CD5 and CD28 on Vδ1 than Vδ2.

> "Compared to Vδ2 Tc, Vδ1 Tc had lower levels of CD3, TCR-γδ, CD5 and CD28, and higher levels of CD8 expression, as evaluated by the MedFI (MWUT and WSRT: p<0.001 in all cases)"
> — Fonseca et al. 2020, DOI: 10.3390/cells9030729

They also report a higher fraction of CD5− and CD28− cells among Vδ1.

> "Vδ1 Tc also had a higher fraction of CD5-and CD28-cells, compared to Vδ2 Tc (MWUT and WSRT: p < 0.001 in both cases)"
> — Fonseca et al. 2020, DOI: 10.3390/cells9030729

This is consistent with the broader literature distinguishing the two subsets by CD5, CD28, and CD57.

> "In addition, it has been found that Vδ2 Tc are mainly CD5+, CD28+ and CD57-, whereas Vδ1 Tc tend to be CD5-/+low, CD28-and CD57+ [189]."
> — Fonseca et al. 2020, DOI: 10.3390/cells9030729
